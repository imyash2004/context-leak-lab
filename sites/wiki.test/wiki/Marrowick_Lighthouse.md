# Marrowick Lighthouse

**Marrowick Lighthouse** is a decommissioned lighthouse on the northern headland of Marrowick, overlooking the entrance to Tessaly Bay.

## History
The lighthouse was built in **1847** to a design by the engineer **Elena Voss**, after a series of wrecks on the Marrowick shoals in the 1830s. Voss chose a tapering granite tower 31 metres tall, with walls 2 metres thick at the base, so that it could survive winter storms from the north-west.

The original oil lamp was replaced by an electric light in 1911. A keeper's cottage and fog-signal house were added in 1903 by the harbour board's engineer, Tomas Ruel, who is sometimes wrongly credited with the tower itself.

The light was automated in 1962 and switched off in 1994, when a modern beacon was installed on the breakwater.

## Today
The tower was restored between 2008 and 2011 and is now a small maritime museum, open from April to October. Visitors can climb the 154 steps to the lantern room.
